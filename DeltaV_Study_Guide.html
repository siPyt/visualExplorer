Chapter 1: The Three Equipment Levels & Scan Heartbeat

In DeltaV, control logic is organized into three clean layers so no single module becomes an unreadable wall of wires. An Equipment Module (EM) never wires directly to physical field terminals—it always talks down to Control Modules (CMs).

Figure 1: DeltaV 3-Tier Equipment Hierarchy — Notice solid wire for Analog (.CV kg) and dashed wires for Discrete commands/states.
The Module Scan (One Heartbeat)
Every module runs on a repeating timer called a Scan (commonly 500 ms or 1 second). On each heartbeat, the module reads all inputs, solves FBD blocks strictly in their Execution Order (#1 → #2 → #3 shown in the bottom-right corner of every block), and writes outputs. Putting math (CALC1) at #1, cutoff check (CND3) at #2, and the close command (ACT) at #3 ensures decisions use fresh numbers from the same scan.

Chapter 2: 1 Outer Algorithm vs. 4 Inner Patterns

This is the single most important mental shortcut for reading any DeltaV EM without getting lost:

1 Outer Algorithm (DeltaV Menu Choice)

When creating an EM in DeltaV Explorer, you pick ONE outer container:

  1. Command-Driven (EM_TANK_CHARGE) — Multi-step SFC driven by A_COMMAND (255 = Idle).
  2. State-Driven — Single-step snap per state (common for valve headers).
  3. FBD — Continuous wire sheet every Scan, zero steps.
  4. SFC — One standalone sequence chart.

4 Inner Patterns (Reading Checklist)

Inside the EM, up to FOUR patterns work together:

  1. Command, State & Step Progression (SFC) — Only pattern that steps through time.
  2. Permissives & Interlocks (FBD) — Always-on start gates & mid-run safety trips.
  3. Mode & Setpoint Routing (FBD) — Always-on weight selection, tare math, & mode push.
  4. CM Command & Feedback (FBD) — Always-on valve/motor commands & limit-switch proof.

Chapter 3: Operating Modes & Signal Health (.CV vs. .ST)

Every DeltaV function block has a Mode that answers one simple question: "Who has the steering wheel right now?"

Mode Full Name Plain-English Meaning in EM_TANK_CHARGE
OOS Out of Service Locked out in software. Stops normal control and stamps .ST = BAD on its outputs so upstream modules refuse to trust it.
MAN Manual Operator has the wheel. A human drives the valve/motor directly from the faceplate; automated commands from the EM are ignored.
AUTO Automatic Self-driven. Follows its own local target typed on its faceplate (does not listen to the EM upstairs).
CAS Cascade Following the boss upstairs. When CM_FEED_XV/DC1 is in CAS, it obeys Open/Close commands written to CAS_IN_D by EM_TANK_CHARGE.
MODE.TARGET vs. MODE.ACTUAL

MODE.TARGET is what you ask for. MODE.ACTUAL is what the block is truly in right now. A field interlock or operator switch can keep MODE.ACTUAL in MAN even if the EM writes MODE.TARGET := CAS. Rule: Always write TARGET, always verify ACTUAL.

.CV (Value) vs. .ST (Health Grade)

Every DeltaV wire carries two pieces of data together: .CV (the live number or state, like 340.5 kg) and .ST (GOOD, UNCERTAIN, or BAD). If the scale wire breaks, .ST turns BAD and Pattern 2 trips immediately instead of trusting a frozen number.

  • Single quotes '...': Required around cross-module paths and local shortcuts in DeltaV Structured Text expressions.
  • The ^/ shortcut: Means "right here inside this same module" (e.g., '^/TARGET_WT.CV' points to TARGET_WT inside EM_TANK_CHARGE).

Chapter 5: Hold, Restart, Abort, Golden Tare & EPH Handshake

1. Permissive vs. Interlock (Wait Peacefully vs. Hit the Brakes)

  • Permissive (Start Gate — CND1): Checked before leaving S0_IDLE (e.g., valves in CAS, tank not already full, scale .ST = GOOD). If false, the EM waits calmly in Idle.
  • Interlock (Mid-Run Trip — CND2 → ACT1): Watched every Scan while running. If triggered (High-High tank level or scale .ST = BAD), it immediately stamps Close onto CM_FEED_XV and forces the EM to HOLD or ABORT.

2. Hold, Restart & Abort — and the Golden Tare Rule

// Captured ONCE on transition S0_IDLE → S10_CHARGE_OPEN (NEVER on Restart!)
'^/START_WT.CV' := 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.CV';
'^/DELIVERED_WT.CV' := 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.CV' - '^/START_WT.CV';
'^/REMAINING_WT.CV' := '^/TARGET_WT.CV' - '^/DELIVERED_WT.CV';
Action What EM_TANK_CHARGE Does What Happens to START_WT.CV?
HOLD Safe pause (operator click or Pattern 2 trip). Immediately closes CM_FEED_XV and pauses step progression. PRESERVED. Still remembers the original pre-charge heel weight.
RESTART After the fault clears and Pattern 2 permissives return, re-opens CM_FEED_XV to finish the remaining weight. NOT OVERWRITTEN! If it re-tared on Restart, it would forget solvent already added and overfill the tank.
ABORT Full emergency stop. Immediately closes CM_FEED_XV, stops CM_AGIT_MTR, and exits the charge command. Requires a full Reset back to S0_IDLE before starting a fresh charge.

3. The Top Boundary: How the EPH (Recipe Boss) Talks to the EM

  1. Acquire (Ownership): The EPH acquires EM_TANK_CHARGE so no other recipe phase or operator fights over the same tank.
  2. Command + Setpoint (Downward): Writes A_COMMAND := Charge into Pattern 1 and passes RECIPE_WT into Pattern 3 (BSEL1).
  3. State Back (Upward): Reads EM_STATE (IDLE, RUNNING, HELD, COMPLETE, ABORTED) so the recipe knows when to advance—or hold the batch.
Real-Plant Troubleshooting Check: Stuck in S20_TRIM_CLOSE?
If the EM commanded Close (CAS_IN_D := Close) but won't advance across T30_SHUT_OK into S30_AGITATE, jump straight to Pattern 4 (CM Command & Feedback). Check 'CM_FEED_XV/DC1/PV_D'—if the valve limit switch still reads Open, CND4 holds T30_SHUT_OK false and CND5 raises VALVE_MISMATCH_ALM so the agitator never starts with a leaking feed valve.

Chapter 6: The 30-Second On-Line Troubleshooting Method

When an operator calls and says "The Equipment Module is stuck," never hunt across wires at random. Read every Command-Driven EM in this exact 3-step sequence:

  1. Pattern 1 (SFC) — Where is it? Find the green active step box.
  2. Transition Crossbar (┼) — What is blocking it? Read the transition directly below the active step—whichever condition is FALSE is the exact bottleneck.
  3. Jump to the Matching FBD Pattern — Why is it false? Check Pattern 2 (Permissive/Trip), Pattern 3 (Setpoint/Cutoff math), or Pattern 4 (CM limit-switch feedback).
Plant Symptom Where It's Waiting FBD Sheet Exact DeltaV Pin Tell
Won't start from Idle S0_IDLE above T10_START_OK Pattern 2 (CND1) 'CM_FEED_XV/DC1/MODE.ACTUAL' left in MAN (not CAS) or scale .ST <> GOOD.
Paused mid-fill, valve shut EM_STATE = HELD in S10_CHARGE_OPEN Pattern 2 (CND2 → ACT1) 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.ST' = BAD or HI_HI_LVL tripped; START_WT.CV stays locked.
Valve told Close, won't mix S20_TRIM_CLOSE above T30_SHUT_OK Pattern 4 (CND4 / CND5) CAS_IN_D = Close, but physical switch PV_D = Open → fires FAIL_ACTIVE after DURATION.

Chapter 7: State-Driven Equipment Modules (EM_XFER_HDR)

Not every group of equipment runs a multi-step sequence over time. When coordinating a multi-valve transfer header or manifold (like EM_XFER_HDR routing solvent to Tank A, Tank B, or CIP Drain), engineers pick the State-Driven outer algorithm instead of Command-Driven.

Command-Driven EM (EM_TANK_CHARGE)

"Do a task over time." Steps sequentially through S10 → S20 → S30 until finished, then returns to Complete/Idle.

State-Driven EM (EM_XFER_HDR)

"Hold an alignment." Each Named Set state snaps directly to a fixed Device Alignment Mask (table of Open/Close & Start/Stop targets).

Figure 3: State-Driven EM_XFER_HDR — Switch to EM_XFER_HDR inside Visual Explorer to test stuck-valve and MAN faults live.
  • EM_S_COMMAND vs. PV_D: EM_S_COMMAND is the desired route requested; PV_D only confirms that state once every child CM's PV_D matches the active mask.
  • FAIL_ACTIVE (Transition Timeout): If a valve is left in MAN or stuck open past DURATION (e.g., 8 s), EM_S_COMMAND <> PV_D sets FAIL_ACTIVE = 1 and keeps CM_XFER_PMP stopped.
  • FAIL_STATE (Safe Fallback): If header pressure 'CM_HDR_PT/AI1/OUT.CV' > 6.0 bar trips, the EM immediately drives all devices to HEADER_CLOSED.

Appendix B: 60-Second Self-Check Quiz

  1. Of the 4 inner patterns inside EM_TANK_CHARGE, which one is the only pattern that steps through time?
  2. You see a dashed blue wire and a solid black wire on a Control Studio FBD sheet. Which carries a tank weight in kg, and which carries a True/False command or status?
  3. Why do you check 'CM_FEED_XV/DC1/MODE.ACTUAL' = CAS instead of checking MODE.TARGET?
  4. If EM_TANK_CHARGE is paused on HOLD halfway through a 500 kg charge and then RESTARTED, should it re-capture START_WT.CV? Why or why not?
  5. What is the difference between a CND block and an ACT block?
  6. In Pattern 4, what two pins on CM_FEED_XV/DC1 do you compare to catch a stuck valve?
  7. When do you choose a State-Driven EM (EM_XFER_HDR) over a Command-Driven EM (EM_TANK_CHARGE)?
  8. In EM_XFER_HDR, why must CM_XFER_PMP wait for CND_PATH_OK before starting?
Answer Key: (1) Pattern 1 (SFC — Command, State & Step Progression); Patterns 2–4 run continuously every Scan. (2) Solid = analog numeric .CV (kg); Dashed = discrete True/False, mode, or status. (3) Because an operator switch or local interlock can block a mode change—MODE.TARGET only shows what was requested, while MODE.ACTUAL proves the block is truly listening to the EM. (4) Never! Re-taring on Restart would erase the solvent already delivered and overfill the tank. (5) CND asks a Yes/No question (outputs OUT_D); ACT is a "write it once" stamp that writes a command/value when triggered. (6) Outbound command CAS_IN_D vs. confirmed limit-switch feedback PV_D. (7) Choose State-Driven for valve headers/manifolds that snap to and hold an alignment mask; choose Command-Driven for multi-step sequential tasks over time. (8) So a stuck-open valve on another tank (e.g., XV102) cannot cross-contaminate batches when the pump starts!
