Chapter 1: The Three Equipment Tiers & The Module Scan Heartbeat

1.1 Why DeltaV Splits Control Into Three Layers (EPH → EM → CM)

If you tried to put batch recipe steps, tank coordination math, safety interlocks, valve limit-switch timers, and raw 4–20 mA sensor scaling inside one giant diagram, every module in the plant would look like an unreadable spiderweb. DeltaV prevents that by dividing responsibility strictly across three tiers:

DeltaV Tier Plain-English Role What It Owns (Concrete Plant Example: Solvent Mix Tank)
EPH
Equipment Phase		 The Recipe Boss Sits at the batch recipe level. Acquires ownership of EM_TANK_CHARGE, sends the command "Charge 500.0 kg of solvent now," and monitors EM_STATE until the EM reports COMPLETE (or HELD / ABORTED).
EM
Equipment Module		 The Team Coordinator Groups related valves, scales, pumps, and motors into one coordinated process task—such as EM_TANK_CHARGE (tank addition) or EM_XFER_HDR (manifold routing). Crucial Rule: An EM almost never wires directly to physical I/O cards; it sends commands down to CMs and reads their status back up.
CM
Control Module		 Single-Device Controller Owns one physical field instrument or actuator: CM_FEED_XV (supply block valve + open/closed limit switches), CM_TANK_WT (vessel load-cell scale transmitter), or CM_AGIT_MTR (tank agitator motor starter + run contact).
Figure 1.1: The Top Boundary (EPH ↔ EM) and Bottom Boundary (EM ↔ CMs) of EM_TANK_CHARGE.

1.2 Module Scan Rate & Explicit Block Execution Order (#1 → #2 → #3)

Every DeltaV module is assigned a Scan Rate (for Equipment Modules, typically 500 ms or 1 second). Once every heartbeat, the controller wakes up the module, reads incoming parameter references, executes its function blocks, and publishes outgoing writes.

Real-Plant Engineering Trap: Screen Position Means NOTHING in Control Studio FBD!
In Control Studio Function Block Diagrams, placing a block higher or farther left on the screen does not make it execute first. DeltaV solves FBD blocks strictly according to the Execution Order badge (#1, #2, #3) in the bottom-right corner of each block:
  • Correct Order: Target Weight Selector BSEL1 (#1) → Remaining Mass Calculator CALC1 (#2) → Cutoff Check CND3 (#3) → Valve Close Action ACT (#4). All four solve in the same millisecond pass of the scan.
  • Reversed Order Mistake: If CND3 is accidentally numbered #1 and CALC1 is numbered #2, CND3 compares last scan's stale weight! On a 1-second scan class with solvent flowing at 10 kg/sec, that single numbering mistake injects 10 kg of dead-time overshoot on every batch!
  • Why PRECLOSE_LEAD Exists: Because DeltaV EMs commonly scan at 500 ms or 1 s—and physical pneumatic block valves take 1–3 seconds to stroke shut—you must command the feed valve closed early by PRECLOSE_LEAD (e.g., 15.0 kg before target) to account for both actuator travel time and scan latency.

Chapter 2: Separating "1 Outer Algorithm" from "4 Inner Patterns"

2.1 Clearing Up the Biggest Point of Confusion: Most of an EM Does NOT Step!

When engineers first read descriptions of an Equipment Module—"In Step 10 open the valve, in Step 20 trim and close, in Step 30 start the agitator"—it sounds as if the entire Equipment Module is a sequence of SFC steps. It is not.

Almost every Equipment Module is built out of four cooperating functional jobs ("Patterns"), and only Pattern 1 steps through time. Patterns 2, 3, and 4 have zero steps—they run continuously on every single Scan like permanent hardwiring:

Zone Inside the EM Steps Like an SFC? How It Actually Executes Inside the Controller
Pattern 1: Command, State & Step Flow YES (The ONLY one!) Walks sequentially through time: S0_IDLE → S10_CHARGE_OPEN → S20_TRIM_CLOSE → S30_AGITATE.
Pattern 2: Permissives & Interlocks NO — Always On Watches High-High level and scale health (.ST) on every single Scan. If the scale fails mid-charge, Pattern 2 slams the valve shut immediately without waiting for an SFC transition!
Pattern 3: Mode & Setpoint Routing NO — Always On Continuously selects active target weight (BSEL1), computes net & remaining mass (CALC1), and pushes CAS mode requests every Scan.
Pattern 4: CM Command & Feedback NO — Always On Acts like permanent cables carrying commands down to valve/motor CMs (CAS_IN_D) and bringing physical limit-switch proof (PV_D) back up every Scan.

Mental Picture: Think of Pattern 1 as the orchestra conductor turning pages of sheet music, while Patterns 2, 3, and 4 are the circuit breakers, mixer board, and speaker cables that stay energized the entire concert.

2.2 "4 Algorithms" vs. "4 Patterns" — The House Floor Plan vs. The Rooms Inside

  • Algorithms (Official DeltaV Menu Setting — Pick ONE): The outer container template you select from a dropdown in DeltaV Explorer when creating the Equipment Module.
  • Patterns (Your Engineering Reading Checklist — Up to ALL FOUR Inside): The four everyday jobs wired inside that container so you can read any Control Studio sheet without getting lost in wires.
Core EM Algorithm Best Plant Use Case How Control Studio Structures It (Watch Out for State-Driven!)
1. Command-Driven
(EM_TANK_CHARGE)		 Timed multi-stage equipment tasks (charging, heating, CIP cycles). Driven by Named Set parameter A_COMMAND (255 reserved for Idle). Each command opens a true multi-step SFC because order and timing matter (open valve → wait for cutoff → confirm closed → agitate).
2. State-Driven
(EM_XFER_HDR)		 Multi-valve headers, manifolds, & routing alignments. DeltaV's Visual Trick: Control Studio opens an SFC-looking window for each state (e.g., Header_Closed, Route_Tank_A), but it does not walk step-by-step—each state holds a single step/mask that flips all valves to their target alignment at once!
3. Function Block Diagram (FBD) Continuous coordination, ratio/split routing, interlock hubs. Pure left-to-right wire sheet of function blocks (CND, ACT, CALC, BSEL) solved every Scan with zero SFC charts or steps.
4. Sequential Function Chart (SFC) Simple standalone single-sequence helpers. One standalone step-by-step SFC sequence for the whole module instead of separate per-command sub-charts.
DeltaV Explorer Menu Heads-Up: Why You'll See 7 Choices in the Dropdown
When creating an Equipment Module Class in DeltaV Explorer, the Algorithm dropdown actually displays 7 items: the 4 core algorithms above plus 3 advanced hybrids (Phase Algorithm, Phase with command-driven run logic, and Phase with state-driven run logic) that embed full batch phase state machines inside an EM. Focus on the core 4 first—Command-Driven and State-Driven power over 90% of real plant Equipment Modules.

2.3 Visual Key: The 5 Standard Shapes Control Studio Draws on Screen

  1. Function Block Box (CND, ACT, CALC, BSEL, DC, AI, PID): Rectangular box with block type centered above, shaded header bar with instance tag (CND1), left-wall input pins, right-wall output pins, and bottom-right execution badge (#1, #2).
  2. Pointed Pentagon Reference Flags (Sheet Margins): Left-margin flags pull external CM signals or internal parameters onto the sheet; right-margin flags push commands/modes out to CMs or SFC transitions.
  3. Solid Orthogonal Wires: Carry continuous analog numeric .CV values (e.g., 500.0 kg, 2.5 bar).
  4. Dashed Orthogonal Wires: Carry discrete True/False values (1/0, Open/Close), mode checks, status checks, and back-calculation handshakes.
  5. SFC Sequence Elements & Action Qualifiers: Double-bordered Initial Step (S0_IDLE), single-bordered Steps (S10..S30), and horizontal Transition crossbars (┼). Unlike generic flowcharts, DeltaV embeds step actions inside each step using qualifiers: [P] Pulse once on step entry, [N] Non-stored (active while in step), [S] Set/latch, [R] Reset, and [D] Delayed.

Chapter 3: Modes, Dual-Data Wires (.CV + .ST), ACT Stamps & Path Syntax

3.1 Operating Modes ("Who Has the Steering Wheel?")

Every controllable DeltaV block (such as DC1 inside CM_FEED_XV or CM_AGIT_MTR) has an operating mode that governs who is allowed to move the physical field equipment:

Mode Full Name Exact Controller Behavior & Effect on Upstream EM
OOS Out of Service Software Lockout. Freezes normal control processing and stamps .ST = BAD on all block outputs so upstream EMs immediately know the device is unavailable.
MAN Manual Operator Has the Wheel. The control room operator sets the valve/motor directly on its faceplate. Any commands written by the EM to CAS_IN_D are completely ignored!
AUTO Automatic Local Faceplate Setpoint. Follows its own local setpoint (SP_D or SP) entered on the CM faceplate—still ignores upstream EM cascade commands.
CAS Cascade Following the Boss Upstairs. Copies the upstream EM's command on CAS_IN_D straight into its active setpoint SP_D. Required for automated EM control!

3.2 Three Platform Realities That Catch Engineers on Live DeltaV Sheets

Reality 1: Always Write MODE.TARGET, Always Check MODE.ACTUAL!
When EM_TANK_CHARGE runs in AUTO, Pattern 3's ACT2 writes 'CM_FEED_XV/DC1/MODE.TARGET' := CAS;. However, a local field interlock, maintenance lock, or operator action inside CM_FEED_XV can force MODE.ACTUAL into MAN or LO (Local Override) even while MODE.TARGET still reads CAS! Therefore, Pattern 2's start permissive (CND1) must check 'CM_FEED_XV/DC1/MODE.ACTUAL' = CAS.
Reality 2: Every DeltaV Wire Carries Status (.ST) Alongside Value (.CV)!
A DeltaV wire is never "just a floating-point number or bit." Every connection carries a two-part structure: Current Value (.CV) + Signal Status (.ST: GOOD, UNCERTAIN, or BAD, plus limit flags). If a load-cell transmitter loses power or CM_TANK_WT/AI1 is placed in OOS, OUT.CV may freeze at 280.0 kg—which looks totally normal numerically! Checking 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.ST' = BAD catches the broken wire on the very next Scan instead of overfilling the vessel blind.
Reality 3: ACT Blocks Stamp & Latch Writes — They Do NOT Auto-Reset!
An ACT (Action) block triggered by a CND block or SFC step executes an assignment statement (:=) and leaves that target parameter at the written value until another ACT block explicitly writes something else. If S10_CHARGE_OPEN writes CAS_IN_D := Open and you forget to write CAS_IN_D := Close in S20_TRIM_CLOSE, HOLD, and ABORT, the valve stays wide open!

Chapter 5: Hold, Restart, Abort & The Golden Tare Math

5.1 Why Net Addition Requires a Tare Snapshot — And How Bad Restart Logic Overfills Tanks

In almost every real batch plant, CM_TANK_WT measures total gross vessel weight (the metal agitator heel + solvent heels + previous recipe ingredients). If the tank already holds 100.0 kg and the recipe calls for adding 500.0 kg of solvent, stopping when the scale reads 500.0 kg would short-charge the batch by 100 kg!

(* Executed ONCE via [P] Pulse on S0_IDLE → S10_CHARGE_OPEN — NEVER on Restart from Hold! *) '^/START_WT.CV' := 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.CV'; (* Locks at 100.0 kg *) '^/DELIVERED_WT.CV' := 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.CV' - '^/START_WT.CV'; '^/REMAINING_WT.CV' := '^/TARGET_WT.CV' - '^/DELIVERED_WT.CV';

Proof by Numbers (Why You NEVER Re-Tare on Restart): Suppose you start at 100 kg heel (START_WT.CV = 100) targeting 500 kg net addition (600 kg final gross). At 300 kg gross (200 kg added), an operator clicks Hold to check a pump strainer, then clicks Restart:

  • Correct DeltaV Design (Tare Preserved): START_WT.CV stays at 100 kg. On Restart, DELIVERED_WT = 300 - 100 = 200 kg, so the EM adds only the remaining 300 kg and stops cleanly at 600 kg.
  • Broken Design (Re-Taring on S10 Entry During Restart): Overwrites START_WT.CV := 300 kg! Now DELIVERED_WT resets to 0 kg, so the EM pumps a brand-new 500 kg on top of 300 kg—slamming the vessel to 800 kg (200 kg overfill!).
CommandImmediate Equipment & Sequence ActionEffect on START_WT.CV & Recovery
HOLDStamps CAS_IN_D := Close on CM_FEED_XV, pauses step progression, reports HELD to EPH.Preserved (Locked). Ready for safe RESTART once fault clears.
RESTARTChecks Pattern 2 permissives (CND1 = True), then re-opens CM_FEED_XV to finish remaining mass.Untouched! Continues subtracting original pre-charge START_WT.CV.
ABORTEmergency stop: closes CM_FEED_XV, stops CM_AGIT_MTR, aborts command sequence.Requires full Reset to S0_IDLE before starting a new charge.

Chapter 6: The 60-Second Control Room Triage Playbook

When an operator calls you over because an Equipment Module is stuck mid-batch, follow this exact four-checkpoint discipline instead of clicking around randomly:

  1. Checkpoint 1 — Pattern 1 Active Step & Transition Crossbar: Open the SFC first. Identify the green active step and read the boolean expression on the red [FALSE] transition crossbar directly underneath it.
  2. Checkpoint 2 — Active Interlocks (Pattern 2 Strip B): If EM_STATE is HELD or ABORTED, look at CND2 / ACT1 to see which signal quality (.ST = BAD) or process limit (HI_HI_LVL) tripped.
  3. Checkpoint 3 — Outbound Command vs. Inbound Proof (Pattern 4): Compare what the EM wrote to CAS_IN_D against what the field limit switches report on PV_D, and check whether a CND confirm timer (DURATION) has flagged a discrepancy / FAIL_ACTIVE alarm.
  4. Checkpoint 4 — Analog Value Movement & Status (Pattern 3): Confirm the sensor .CV is physically changing in the expected direction and isn't frozen in manual simulation or UNCERTAIN / BAD status.

Chapter 7: State-Driven Equipment Modules — EM_XFER_HDR

7.1 How a State-Driven Manifold Works Inside Control Studio

7.2 Three Critical Protections Wired Into Every Industrial State-Driven Header

  1. Anti-Cross-Contamination Pump Gate (CND_PATH_OK → ACT_PMP_GATE): Even though ROUTE_TANK_A calls for running CM_XFER_PMP, the pump is held at Stop until CND_PATH_OK (#1) proves via limit switches (PV_D) that XV101 = Open and opposing valves XV102 & XV103 are physically Closed. Otherwise, a stuck-open Tank B valve would pump Tank A's batch straight into Tank B!
  2. Deadhead Overpressure Fallback (CND_HDR_HI → ACT_FAIL_STATE): Monitors solid analog wire 'CM_HDR_PT/AI1/OUT.CV' every Scan. If line pressure exceeds 6.0 bar (or .ST = BAD), it immediately forces the entire header to its safe FAIL_STATE (HEADER_CLOSED).
  3. Confirmed State Decoder & Timeout (CALC_PV_STATE → CND_FAIL_ALM): CALC_PV_STATE (#1) decodes all four live PV_D feedbacks into confirmed header state ^/PV_D. If ^/EM_S_COMMAND <> ^/PV_D longer than DURATION = 8s, CND_FAIL_ALM (#2) sets FAIL_ACTIVE = 1 to alert the operator and upstream EPH.

Detailed Engineering Answer Key

1. Because FBD solves strictly in #1 → #2 order—CND3 (#1) would evaluate last scan's stale weight, adding one full EM scan period (0.5–1.0 s) of unmeasured liquid flow before closing.

2. Only Pattern 1 (SFC) steps through time; Patterns 2, 3, and 4 solve continuously on every Scan even in Idle.

3. An operator switch or local CM interlock can hold MODE.ACTUAL in MAN or LO even while MODE.TARGET requests CAS—which is why permissives always check MODE.ACTUAL.

4. Every wire carries .ST alongside .CV; Pattern 2's CND2 detects 'CM_TANK_WT/AI1/OUT.ST' = BAD on the very next Scan and triggers ACT1 to close the valve and force HELD.

5. 800 kg gross (200 kg overfill!) — overwriting START_WT.CV := 300 erases the 200 kg already added and adds another full 500 kg on top of 300 kg.

6. A 2-state valve only has Close/Open, so S10 must close early by PRECLOSE_LEAD and S20 becomes pure settle/shut verification; a Bulk+Dribble assembly uses a 3-state Named Set (Close/Dribble/Bulk) on DC1 to physically throttle in S20.

7. A State-Driven EM displays an SFC-looking window per state, but does not step through time—each state holds one single alignment mask that snaps all devices at once.

8. Check the Device Alignment Mask (Tab 1) and CND_PATH_OK (Tab 2): an opposing valve (like Tank B's CM_HDR_XV102 or drain XV103) is failing to prove PV_D = Closed (stuck open), holding CND_PATH_OK = 0 to prevent cross-contamination!
